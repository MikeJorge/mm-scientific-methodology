Learning & application · 3 of 3

Can a new reader apply the method?

Observe what a developer can explain, decide and justify on a bounded task.

A documentation review can identify clarity and consistency. Observing an external developer apply the method supplies a different kind of evidence: what the reader understood, what was omitted and where assistance was needed.

Protocol prepared · evaluation not yet run

Human participants observed: 0. Empirical adoption effectiveness: not established. The executable example is a separate didactic check; it is not a participant study.

A small transfer task

Give a developer who did not construct MM a proposed change to the period-deficit report: permit an incomplete report when one period has no observation, while keeping known deficits and the uncertainty visible. The existing contract rejects missing observations, so the reader must address a real contract change rather than repeat the completed example.

The task supplies demand of 10 per period and supply [10,14,null,8,10,10,10,10,10,10,10,10]. P03 is missing and P04 has a recorded value. The participant proposes requirements, evidence and a bounded decision. Production coding and scientific model execution are outside the task.

Five competencies to observe

  1. Need and decisions. Explain the user's need and distinguish domain/scientific judgment from a programming task.
  2. Contracts and traceability. Identify relevant modules, contracts and requirements, including what would change.
  3. Two assessment plans. Define verification against a specification and validation/adequacy against ConOps, users and conditions.
  4. Prospective acceptance. State criteria and expected cases before inspecting implementation results.
  5. Limits and a justified decision. Recognize missing evidence or specialist judgment and justify acceptance, rejection or a hold within the available evidence.

A reproducible session sequence

  1. Record the conditions. Relevant experience, MM familiarity, prior answer exposure, allowed tools/AI, selected material identities and assistance rules.
  2. Present the task and agreed references. Use the participant sheet. For an unassisted transfer observation, withhold the worked answer and assessor discussion until the first response is preserved.
  3. Observe and freeze the first response. Record questions, omissions and help; save the response with timestamp and hash before feedback.
  4. Optionally support a second attempt. Provide the example or hints, then preserve a separate assisted revision.
  5. Assess and report the scope. Apply the five-part rubric and identify concrete difficulties tied to the exact material.

The example and assessor guidance are public. Previous or accidental exposure must be recorded; public materials do not establish blinding. An exposed or assisted session can still be useful, provided its conditions are accurately described.

Rubric and scoped outcomes

Rate each competency as demonstrated, partially demonstrated or not demonstrated, citing the response evidence. Record assistance separately as none, procedural or substantive. Do not use a total score that allows a central V&V or missingness error to be compensated by other strengths.

OutcomeMeaning
Completed independently for this taskAll five competencies demonstrated in the frozen first response without substantive assistance, with exposure and tool conditions reported.
Completed with assistance for this taskAll five demonstrated only after substantive help; first and revised responses remain distinguishable.
Not demonstrated for this taskOne or more competencies remain partial, the session is incomplete or the evidence is insufficient. Identify the specific gap.

A single pilot can reveal local adoption difficulties. It cannot establish that all mid-level or senior developers can use the methodology, nor validate this observation protocol as a general measurement instrument.

Assessor discussion — view after the first response

A coherent proposal reports a known deficit of 2 at P04, preserves P03 as unknown and prevents a claim that all twelve periods have been fully assessed. An incomplete report can support a narrower decision if that use is explicitly agreed. Complete adequacy remains unresolved until the missing period or its treatment is justified.

Verification examines the revised contract and implementation. Adequacy assessment examines whether the incomplete report serves the analyst's intended decision under the declared conditions. The assessor should accept equivalent sound designs, not require one particular output schema or memorized DM identifier.

Relationship to the Manual

DM-0155 provides a related fresh-reader check for documentation of workflow reproduction. This smaller comprehension task is inspired by that practice; it does not demonstrate restoration of the complete scientific workflow. DM-0158 is a conditional SHOULD for executable or tested documentation examples when feasible and scientifically safe. Neither record is amended by this addition.
